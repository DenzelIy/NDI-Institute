About the Institute

The Niger Delta Institute of Digital Technology is the awarding arm of Niger Delta Innovate Ltd/Gte, a company limited by guarantee working to close the digital skills gap across the Niger Delta.

Niger Delta Innovate already runs digital technology teaching in secondary schools against the national curriculum, Technovation Clubs in coding, artificial intelligence and robotics, and RAIL, the Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory at Okrika Grammar School. The Institute exists because those students, and the adults who watched them, need somewhere to take the next step that ends in a qualification rather than a certificate of attendance.

How we hold the standard

An award is only worth what stands behind it. This is what stands behind ours.

Every assessment is moderated before it is used

An internal moderator approves each assessment instrument, dated, before it reaches a student. An external moderator reviews a sample from every intake, and that sample must include every fail and every distinction. Results are ratified by the Programmes and Awards Committee, and no certificate is awarded any other way.

A course file for every course, every intake

The specification, the teaching materials, every assessment with its mark scheme, the moderation approvals, the mark sheet, the moderated sample, the external report, the committee minute, and what students said about the course. An incomplete file is reported to the Committee by name.

We are open about AI, and closed where it counts

Every assessment states whether AI use is open, assistive or closed. We teach the tools rather than pretend they do not exist. The capstone is defended in person before two assessors, which is the assessment that cannot be delegated, purchased or generated.

Nothing is advertised before it is ready

A programme opens only when its specification is approved, its instructor is appointed with a complete qualification file, and its equipment is confirmed in place. That is why fourteen certificates open in three groups rather than all at once.

Governance

The Institute is a unit of Niger Delta Innovate and answers to its Board of Directors.

  • Board of DirectorsOpening or closing a programme, tuition and scholarship policy, the Institute budget, and the authority under which certificates are executed.
  • Programmes and Awards CommitteeThe academic authority: approves specifications and assessments, ratifies results, awards certificates, appoints the external moderator, and decides academic misconduct outcomes. It meets at the opening and close of every intake and is minuted every time.
  • RegistryApplications, admissions, registration, attendance, the marks database, the moderation trail, certificates, transcripts and the archive.
  • Quality AssuranceSpecifications, course files, lesson observation, the student voice, external moderation, and all regulatory correspondence. Reports quality failures directly to the Executive Director.

Safeguarding is not a function but a named person, and applicants under 18 are covered by our safeguarding policy from the moment they apply.

Niger Delta Innovate

Schools and clubs

Digital technology taught to the national curriculum from JSS1 to SS3 in partner schools, with Technovation Clubs in coding, artificial intelligence and robotics running to a single shared schedule across every school.

RAIL

The Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory at Okrika Grammar School, in service since March 2026 and the first facility of its kind in Rivers State. It is where robotics and hardware teaching happens, and where research and prototype work is built.

Loop

Our own learning and assessment platform. It carries course delivery, assessment and the student record, and it is the system of record for the Institute rather than a filing cabinet with a login.

Recognition, stated plainly

Programmes lead to a certificate awarded by Niger Delta Innovate Ltd/Gte. We are in the process of seeking recognition from the National Board for Technical Education. Recognition is not guaranteed and no date can be given.

Our curriculum is mapped to the National Skills Qualification framework as a design decision. We do not describe any programme as accredited, nationally recognised or government-approved, and we will not do so until a certificate of recognition is in our hands.
